\section{Literature Analysis}

inductive thematic analysis

and analyse the included publications using thematic analysis~\cite{using_thematic_analysis_2006}. As the starting point for our synthesis, we construct the concept matrix proposed by~\cite{webster_watson_literature_review_2002}. For each publication, we use the matrix to record passages describing challenges caused by using NAS in FL, proposed solutions and the relevant FL system characteristics. The matrix preserves the link between publications and concepts while organising the synthesis around recurring challenges and solutions instead of individual NAS-in-FL methods.

We apply the six stages of thematic analysis through three analytical iterations inspired by~\cite{smart_contracts_challenges_solutions_2022} and~\cite{cdml_2024}. In the first iteration, we familiarise ourselves with the complete literature set and populate the concept matrix with initial semantic codes. Each code records the meaning of a passage describing an FL system characteristic, a challenge or a solution without assigning it to a predetermined theme.

In the second iteration, we search for themes by comparing codes across publications and collating conceptually equivalent descriptions. Related codes form recurring challenges and solution types, which we arrange into candidate subthemes and overarching themes. A theme captures a repeated pattern relevant to the research question, while a subtheme distinguishes a coherent pattern within a broader theme. We retain patterns for which the literature provides sufficient evidence to define their meaning and applicability.

In the third iteration, we review the candidate themes against both their coded passages and the complete literature set. We express each challenge as one central analytical claim and merge challenges when their codes describe the same claim in different contexts. We retain separate challenges when they describe independently meaningful relationships and split a challenge when its propositions can hold independently. This applies internal homogeneity within challenges and external heterogeneity between them~\cite{using_thematic_analysis_2006}. We then define and name each theme through its central concept.

We map a solution to a challenge only when its mechanism acts on the cause, process or consequence expressed by the challenge. We distinguish primary responses from secondary effects and record separately whether the relationship is evaluated, claimed by the publication or inferred from the mechanism. A solution may address several challenges, although it is presented with its primary challenge and cross-referenced where necessary. This mapping connects the recurring solutions to the applicable FL system characteristics and NAS approaches without treating their co-occurrence in one publication as evidence of a relationship.
