\chapter{Related Work}\label{chapter:related-work}

Existing NAS-in-FL research addresses six broad purposes: coordinating federated search, reducing system costs, differentiating client architectures, protecting the search process, extending the optimization setting, and applies NAS in FL to specific domains.

General search-process research distributes candidate training and evaluation across clients while coordinating the search at the server \cite{dfnas_2020,efficient_model_training_fednas_2024,federated_bayesian_optimization_nas_2023,fednas_2021,rl_fednas_2021,rt_fed_evo_nas_2020}. These publications establish complete NAS-in-FL methods that often combine several coordination mechanisms.

Resource-oriented research reduces search and deployment costs using methods tailored to computational, communication, and device constraints \cite{decnas_2022,fednas_over_hetero_mobile_devices_2022,divide-and-conquer_fednas_2023,efnas_2024,f2nas_2024,fedoras_2022,fgfl_2024,finch_2024,network_aware_fed_nas_2025,optimize_fednas_edge_2021,rafl_2024,superfednas_2024,supernet_trade-off_fednas_2025}. Their applicability depends on which cost is constrained.

Personalization research searches architectures at different levels of the federation and coordinates heterogeneous client models \cite{clustered_direct_fednas_2022,collaborative_nas_for_pfl_2025,enasfl_2024,fedpnas_2021,hafl_2022,hapfnas_2025,hfedghn_2023,peaches_2024,perfedrlnas_2024,spider_2021}. This work separates the goal of differentiated architectures from the problem of coordinating them.

Security research uses privacy-preserving and robust mechanisms to protect search information and models \cite{dp-fnas_2020,fednas_based_attack_2024,hetefed_2023,multi-granular_fednas_2023,privacy-preserving_nas_across_fl_iot_2021,privacy_preserving_fednas_for_edge_2025}. The available protection mechanisms depend on the representation exchanged during search.

Further work extends NAS-in-FL to broader objectives, model classes, and federated learning settings \cite{aging_fednas_2025,apons_2023,feathers_2023,fed_meta_nas_2025,fednaspet_2023,fl-agnns_2023,gpt4-boosted_pso_fednas_2024,multi_objective_evo_fl_2020,nasfly_2024,pfgnas_2025,self-supervised_vertical_fednas_2023}. These extensions introduce assumptions that restrict transfer to other settings.

Application research evaluates NAS-in-FL across a range of specialized domains \cite{breastcancer_fednas_2024,cit2fr-fl-nas_2022,electricity_load_forecasting_fl_darts_2023,energy_fednas_2025,epistatis_detection_fuzzy_fednas_2025,fed_auto_mri_2023,fedregnas_2025,intrusion_detection_fednas_2024,load_monitoring_fednas_2025,medical_image_segmentation_fednas_2024,mri_generalizable_reconstruction_fednas_2025,prostate_mri_fednas_2021,remote_sensing_fednas_2025,sfnas-ddpg_2024,supervised_fednas_2023,wind_power_prediction_fednas_2024}. It demonstrates domain breadth, although specialized evaluations provide limited evidence about mechanism reuse elsewhere.

\section{Prior Syntheses of NAS-in-FL}

One early review classifies complete methods by online and offline execution or by single- and multi-objective search \cite{fl_to_nas_survey_2021}. Broader reviews compare NAS with hyperparameter optimization or place architecture search within multi-objective FL \cite{nas_hpo_fl_survey_2023,multi-objective_methods_in_fl_2025}. These classifications organize procedures and objectives rather than the relationship between challenge and solution.

The most similar synthesis decomposes NAS-in-FL into design elements, method archetypes, traits, and suitable FL systems \cite{nas_in_fl_2026}. General NAS and FL reviews supply complementary concepts for search design and federated constraints \cite{nas_survey_2019,fl_taxonomy_2024}. Their units remain complete methods, NAS components, or FL techniques.

\section{Position of This Review}

This thesis instead synthesizes recurring challenges and solution mechanisms. It separates solution mechanisms that are part of complete NAS-in-FL methods and maps their many-to-many relationships with challenges. This perspective supports assessing reuse in a specified NAS-in-FL scenario without claiming that unevaluated combinations will work.
