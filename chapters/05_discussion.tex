\section{Principal Findings}

The findings show that identifying a suitable solution for using NAS in FL requires more than matching a named method to a general challenge. Limited client resources, heterogeneity, architecture-search coordination, and security affect different parts of the search and become relevant under different FL system characteristics. Their effects also overlap. A client's bandwidth can limit the feasible search state, unequal bandwidth can bias which data inform candidate evaluation, and the resulting structural differences can complicate aggregation. The research problem is therefore best understood as a conditional mapping between a system context, the challenge created in that context, and a compatible solution. This complements method-level classifications and archetypes, which explain how complete NAS-in-FL methods are designed but do not by themselves establish which component is suitable for a particular problem \cite{nas_in_fl_2026}.

Resource constraints illustrate why this contextual distinction matters. On weak cross-device clients, local supernet training may exceed compute or memory budgets. Single-path training, progressive pruning, lightweight search spaces, and nested weight sharing reduce this local burden \cite{dfnas_2020,peaches_2024,load_monitoring_fednas_2025}. Candidate-based searches face a different cost because every evaluation can invoke several FL rounds. Limiting fine-tuning, predicting performance, evaluating candidates in parallel, or inheriting supernet weights reduces repeated evaluation, while weight inheritance and one-stage optimisation can avoid a separate retraining stage \cite{decnas_2022,hapfnas_2025,medical_image_segmentation_fednas_2024}. A shared supernet can further serve several deployment targets, and split-model searches can use otherwise idle client time \cite{superfednas_2024,nasfly_2024}. Each solution has different preconditions: a restricted search space requires domain knowledge, parallel evaluation requires enough available clients, and inherited weights provide a cheaper but potentially less faithful ranking than full candidate training.

The communication findings reveal a similar separation between message size, synchronisation frequency, and the communication cost induced by the selected model. Compression and compact architecture encodings address oversized search messages; infrequent or hierarchical aggregation addresses repeated wide-area synchronisation; and communication-aware objectives prevent the search from producing a model that remains expensive in later FL rounds \cite{fedregnas_2025,finch_2024,multi_objective_evo_fl_2020}. Compact encodings are applicable only when clients already share the search space and weights. Hierarchical aggregation presupposes suitable intermediate infrastructure. Consequently, a cross-silo system with capable servers but expensive inter-organisational links may need communication solutions without the compute reductions required by a mobile fleet.

Heterogeneity and unreliability show that feasibility and representativeness must be considered. Matching subnets or workloads to client capacity, grouping clients by latency, tolerating stale updates, and keeping the full search model at the server can prevent weak clients from delaying the search \cite{fedoras_2022,nasfly_2024,rl_fednas_2021,rafl_2024}. However, clients then train different portions or candidates. Exposure-aware aggregation is needed where parameters overlap, while tier-specific architectures, constraint-specific subnet selection, and hardware-aware objectives address heterogeneous deployment budgets \cite{fgfl_2024,clustered_direct_fednas_2022,superfednas_2024}. When availability and connectivity correlate with non-IID data, selecting only fast clients can make candidate comparisons reflect client assignment rather than architecture quality. Unequal sample volumes change estimate reliability and client influence on shared architecture decisions \cite{fednas_2021}. Forming groups from both network conditions and data coverage, or transferring aggregated knowledge across candidate boundaries, preserves evidence \cite{network_aware_fed_nas_2025,finch_2024,hapfnas_2025}. Partial participation addresses dropout, whereas independent stopping criteria address clients whose architecture variables converge at different times \cite{optimize_fednas_edge_2021,collaborative_nas_for_pfl_2025}. Treating these as separate contexts prevents a generic ``asynchronous'' solution from obscuring their different causes. Insufficient capacity bounds what a client can execute, whereas unequal capacity changes whose evidence influences the search; reducing workload addresses the former but may in turn intensify the latter.

Architecture-search coordination depends most directly on the representation of search knowledge. Differentiable searches with a common supernet can aggregate model and architecture parameters together, but this requires identical meanings for every aggregated variable \cite{fednas_2021,dfnas_2020}. Controller-based searches instead have to persist their learned policy, while searches whose architecture quality depends on FL hyperparameters must alternate or jointly optimise these coupled decisions \cite{efficient_model_training_fednas_2024,feathers_2023,aging_fednas_2025}. Regularisation or warm-up and recovery rounds can stabilise structural decisions, depending on whether instability arises in layer-wise gradients or progressive pruning \cite{remote_sensing_fednas_2025,spider_2021}. Vertical FL forms a distinct context because no feature holder can evaluate an end-to-end candidate alone. Exchanging intermediate representations supplies a joint optimisation signal, and self-supervised local pretraining makes unaligned samples useful before coordinated search on the aligned subset \cite{self-supervised_vertical_fednas_2023}.

Security completes the mapping by distinguishing integrity from confidentiality. A malicious client can influence persistent architecture decisions, for which suppressing poisoned operations or restricting the search space provides reported protection \cite{privacy_preserving_fednas_for_edge_2025}. An honest-but-curious party presents another problem: architecture gradients, discrete choices, rewards, and intermediate representations remain data-dependent even though raw data stay local. Differential privacy, secure aggregation of a uniform shared search, and low-dimensional discrete encodings protect different observable search signals \cite{dp-fnas_2020,feathers_2023,multi-granular_fednas_2023}. Their applicability follows the threat model and search representation, and privacy noise or a restricted representation can weaken the evidence guiding architecture selection.

\section{Implications for Practice and Research}

- clear guidance on existing related solutions and challenges

For practitioners, the findings support a diagnostic sequence. The intended system should first be characterised by FL partitioning, client compute and memory, link capacity, participation, data distributions, trust assumptions, and deployment budgets. The search representation can then be examined to locate the constrained or exposed state. Only after these conditions are explicit can the relevant solutions be combined. For example, capacity-specific subnets may require exposure-aware aggregation, representative client grouping, compact communication, and protection of architecture updates. Selecting one component without its dependencies can transfer cost, bias, or exposure to another part of the search. This makes the map useful before implementation because it reveals which assumptions must hold for a solution to remain operationally feasible.

For research, this synthesis suggests that solution configurations are a more informative unit of comparison than complete NAS-in-FL methods. It provides a vocabulary for distinguishing where a federated condition affects the search process, the evidence used to guide it, or the architecture eventually deployed. It also exposes dependencies between solution components that method-level comparisons can conceal. The mapping therefore supports condition-specific comparison rather than a universal ranking of solutions. Its main implication is that the applicable context should form part of every challenge--solution claim.

\section{Limitations}

- rely on literature for facts

The literature search limits the coverage of the synthesis. It uses an English-language Scopus query and one iteration of backward and forward citation searching. Publications using different terminology, indexed elsewhere, or published after the search may therefore be absent. Excluding surveys and publications without enough methodological detail improves the evidential basis for coding, but it can omit challenges or solutions reported only at a high level. The identified concepts should consequently be understood as a synthesis of the reviewed corpus rather than an exhaustive account of every possible NAS-in-FL design.

The thematic analysis introduces interpretative limitations. The coding captures how authors describe and motivate challenges and solutions, and the applicability of a solution sometimes has to be inferred from the reported FL configuration or experimental setup. Decisions to merge conceptually similar mechanisms and separate superficially similar challenges are grounded in the coded extracts, but alternative researchers could draw theme boundaries differently. Repeated comparison with the complete corpus provides an audit trail for these decisions without eliminating the researcher judgement inherent in familiarisation, coding, and theme refinement \cite{using_thematic_analysis_2006}.

Finally, a relationship in the mapping indicates that the literature presents a solution as addressing a challenge under particular conditions. It does not establish the size of that effect or prove that the solution will transfer to another system. The reviewed studies differ in datasets, search spaces, client populations, hardware, resource models, and evaluation budgets. These differences prevent a quantitative comparison across solutions and make publication frequency unsuitable as a measure of effectiveness, severity, or practical prevalence. The findings can therefore guide conditional selection and hypothesis formation, but they cannot guarantee performance in an unstudied FL context.

\section{Future Research}

Future research should test the challenge--solution relationships under controlled and comparable conditions. A shared evaluation framework could vary client compute and memory, bandwidth, data distributions, participation, and deployment budgets while holding the learning task and search space constant. Evaluations should distinguish search cost from the cost of training and deploying the selected architecture. They should also report wall-clock time, transferred bytes, candidate-ranking fidelity, participating-client coverage, final-model quality, and the assumed threat model. These measurements would reveal which system characteristic explains a solution's benefit and where its reported applicability ceases.

The interactions between solutions require particular attention. Resource-aware workload assignment can keep weak clients involved while changing their exposure to supernet operations; compact or noisy search signals can reduce communication or leakage while weakening architecture evidence; and personalised deployment can require a compatible aggregation mechanism. Factorial experiments could evaluate these components individually and in combination, including configurations that address several challenges simultaneously. Such comparisons would show whether their benefits accumulate, interfere, or shift costs to another part of the search.

Further studies should evaluate NAS in FL under combinations of conditions that occur together in operational systems. Dropout, stale updates, unequal connectivity, and non-IID data can jointly determine which clients influence architecture selection. Security evaluations should likewise consider both the predictive model and the integrity of the structural decision, while stating which search artefacts an adversary can observe or manipulate. Evidence from cross-device, cross-silo, and vertical FL deployments would test whether relationships derived largely from individual experimental settings remain useful at operational scale.

Future reviews can extend the database coverage, repeat citation searching, involve multiple coders, and validate the challenge definitions with practitioners responsible for deployed FL systems. A common reporting scheme for client characteristics, search-time resources, deployment constraints, participation, architecture overlap, and security assumptions would make new proposals easier to compare. With stronger comparative evidence, the present mapping could support a decision aid that recommends solution configurations for an explicitly described FL system.
