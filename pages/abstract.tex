\chapter{\abstractname}

Neural architecture search (NAS) automates the design of neural network architectures, while federated learning (FL) enables clients to train models collaboratively without centralizing their data. Combining them can tailor architectures to federated tasks and devices, but distributing architecture search introduces constraints that centralized NAS does not face. Existing research proposes many solutions, yet their mechanisms are commonly embedded within complete NAS-in-FL methods. This makes it difficult to determine which mechanisms have been proposed in response to which challenges.

To consolidate this knowledge, this thesis examined how solutions address recurring challenges in the NAS-in-FL literature. We conducted a concept-centric literature review and thematic analysis of 48 publications identified through a Scopus search and backward and forward citation searching. For each publication, we extracted NAS method types, FL system characteristics, challenges, and distinct solution mechanisms. We mapped a mechanism to a challenge only when a source publication presented that relationship and recorded reported prerequisites and trade-offs as context.

The analysis identified ten challenges across five themes: client resource constraints, client heterogeneity, decentralized search state, participation dynamics, and search security. Across these challenges, we synthesized 34 solution mechanisms. The resulting map makes many-to-many relationships reported in the literature visible: several mechanisms are linked to more than one challenge, and publications describe additional prerequisites, costs, or interactions. Separating mechanisms from complete methods provides a structured account of how the literature responds to NAS-in-FL challenges. The synthesis organizes reported evidence; it does not establish whether a challenge will arise or a mechanism will succeed in an unevaluated configuration. Because the reviewed evaluations cover heterogeneous and often incompletely described configurations, they also do not support a ranking of effectiveness. Controlled empirical evaluations under explicit operating conditions remain necessary.
